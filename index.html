What's being simulated
  • Every bee is an individual with its own age, physiological wear, job and foraging memory; every cell (~140,000 in two deep boxes) holds its own egg, larva, pupa, nectar, honey or bee-bread. The comb view shows a live sample of the bees on the selected frame.
  • Brood development: worker 3 d egg → larva capped day 9 → emerges day 21; drone 3 / 10 / 24 d; queen 3 / 8 / 16 d. Larvae are fed by nurse bees (≈3 larvae per nurse) using pollen and honey; underfed larvae are removed.
  • Age polyethism: cell cleaning → nursing → food storing, comb building & capping → guarding/fanning → foraging at ~3 weeks. Forager age shifts with colony need (social inhibition by older foragers).
  • Lifespan follows workload: summer bees wear out in ~5–6 weeks (foragers die after ~800 km of flight or from predators), while autumn-reared "winter bees" with little brood to feed live 4–6 months.
  • Foraging: foragers fly at ~25 km/h, carry ~40 mg nectar or ~16 mg pollen, and burn sugar in flight. Profitable patches are advertised by waggle dances — the angle from vertical equals the angle from the sun's azimuth, and the waggle run lengthens with distance; nearby sources get short round dances. Recruits follow dances; scouts discover new flowers; poor patches are abandoned. Pollen foraging tracks brood demand.
  • Honey making: nectar (30–45 % sugar) is handed to receiver bees, stored, and fanned down to ~18 % water, then capped with wax. Long unloading queues raise the dance threshold (tremble-dance effect).
  • Seasons & weather (~45°N): real sun position and day length, daily temperature and rain. No flight below ~12 °C, at night or in rain. Bees cluster below ~14 °C; brood that too few bees can cover is chilled. Brood rearing stops in late autumn, drones are evicted, and the colony overwinters on stored honey.
  • Queen biology: laying up to ~1,800 eggs/day by season, pollen and nurse supply; drone eggs in drone comb in spring/summer. Crowding triggers swarming (old queen leaves with ~half the bees), virgin queens emerge, kill rivals, take mating flights, and start laying. Queen loss triggers emergency queen cells; failure leads to laying workers.
  • Not modelled: varroa & disease, robbing, afterswarms, multiple-colony competition, propolis.
